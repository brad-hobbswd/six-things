The Seven Things

Chapter 3: The Old Home Folks and the Church We Inherited

Tradition, Generational Authority, and the Responsibility to Examine What We Pass Down

Every generation inherits something from the people who came before them. Families pass down values, communities preserve customs, and churches establish traditions that shape the spiritual experiences of those who grow up within their congregations. Much of what previous generations provided deserves appreciation, particularly the sacrifices made to preserve faith, maintain fellowship, and establish places where people could worship together. The prayers of grandmothers, the dedication of longtime pastors, the service of church mothers, and the commitment of ordinary believers helped shape countless religious communities. However, appreciating what previous generations accomplished does not require accepting every practice they established without examination.

The phrase the old home folks represents more than a particular age group. It reflects a familiar culture within traditional church environments where longtime members, established families, pastors, elders, and respected leaders often held considerable influence over the congregation’s expectations and identity. Their experiences frequently shaped what was considered acceptable, how disagreements were handled, and which behaviors were regarded as evidence of spiritual maturity. In many settings, their influence provided stability and continuity, allowing congregations to preserve meaningful customs and pass biblical instruction to younger believers. Nevertheless, experience and influence must remain accountable to Scripture, because the length of time someone has practiced a tradition does not establish whether the tradition is consistent with God’s Word.

One of the challenges facing traditional religious communities is the difficulty of distinguishing biblical instruction from cultural expectations that have gradually acquired religious significance. Certain practices may have originated as personal convictions, community preferences, or responses to particular historical circumstances. Over time, those practices can become so familiar that questioning them feels equivalent to questioning God Himself. Yet Scripture does not establish every inherited custom as a divine command, and believers must exercise discernment when determining whether a practice reflects biblical instruction or simply represents the preferences of a particular generation.

Jesus addressed the tension between religious tradition and divine instruction in Mark 7:6–13. He confronted religious leaders who had allowed their traditions to interfere with the commandments of God. His words established an important principle: human traditions must never be elevated above the authority of Scripture. The concern was not that every tradition was inherently harmful, but that religious customs could become a substitute for genuine obedience when people valued established practices more than the principles God had given them. This distinction remains necessary whenever churches evaluate the beliefs and expectations passed from one generation to another.

Mark 7:6–13 · KJV
6 He answered and said unto them, Well hath Esaias prophesied of you hypocrites, as it is written, This people honoureth me with their lips, but their heart is far from me.
7 Howbeit in vain do they worship me, teaching for doctrines the commandments of men.
8 For laying aside the commandment of God, ye hold the tradition of men, as the washing of pots and cups: and many other such like things ye do.
9 And he said unto them, Full well ye reject the commandment of God, that ye may keep your own tradition.
10 For Moses said, Honour thy father and thy mother; and, Whoso curseth father or mother, let him die the death:
11 But ye say, If a man shall say to his father or mother, It is Corban, that is to say, a gift, by whatsoever thou mightest be profited by me; he shall be free.
12 And ye suffer him no more to do ought for his father or his mother;
13 Making the word of God of none effect through your tradition, which ye have delivered: and many such like things do ye.

Generational authority also deserves careful consideration. Older believers often possess experiences and perspectives that younger members have not yet developed, and their wisdom can provide valuable guidance. Scripture encourages respect for those who have lived faithfully and instructs believers to honor those deserving of respect. Leviticus 19:32 specifically teaches reverence toward the elderly, connecting respect for age with the fear of God. However, respect does not eliminate the responsibility to evaluate conduct according to biblical principles. The same Scriptures that encourage younger believers to receive instruction also require those who teach and lead to demonstrate integrity, humility, and righteousness.

Leviticus 19:32 · KJV
32 Thou shalt rise up before the hoary head, and honour the face of the old man, and fear thy God: I am the LORD.

The responsibility of spiritual leadership is especially important because influence can shape the direction of an entire congregation. Pastors, church mothers, elders, and deacons often become trusted voices within their communities, and their words can carry considerable weight. Such influence should encourage careful stewardship rather than personal superiority. First Peter 5:2–3 instructs those who shepherd God’s people to serve willingly and not exercise authority as though they were masters over those entrusted to their care. The passage establishes leadership as a responsibility of service, requiring conduct that reflects the character of the One being represented.

A church’s history should therefore be approached with both gratitude and discernment. Some traditions deserve preservation because they strengthen fellowship, encourage prayer, support families, and keep communities connected to their spiritual heritage. Other practices may require reconsideration when they place unnecessary burdens upon people, discourage legitimate questions, or allow personal preferences to function as religious requirements. The purpose of examining these practices is not to erase the past, but to understand what should continue and what should change. A congregation demonstrates respect for its history when it preserves what is good while remaining willing to correct what has departed from biblical instruction.

The responsibility does not rest exclusively upon older members. Younger believers must also recognize that rejecting tradition simply because it is traditional does not automatically produce spiritual maturity. Wisdom is not determined by age, and neither is righteousness. A younger person can demonstrate pride, dishonesty, and disregard for instruction just as easily as someone who has spent decades within a congregation. Each generation must therefore resist the temptation to establish its own moral superiority over another. The biblical standard remains consistent, and every believer must be willing to submit personal convictions and conduct to that standard.

The seven things identified in Proverbs 6:16–19 provide an important framework for examining the ways generational influence can affect church relationships. Pride can become attached to longstanding positions, dishonesty can be protected through loyalty to familiar personalities, and discord can develop when differences are handled through personal alliances rather than truth and reconciliation. These behaviors are not characteristics of an entire generation, nor should they be attributed to someone merely because they belong to a traditional congregation. Their presence must be established through actual conduct rather than assumptions about age, background, or religious experience.

Proverbs 6:16–19 · KJV
16 These six things doth the LORD hate: yea, seven are an abomination unto him:
17 A proud look, a lying tongue, and hands that shed innocent blood,
18 An heart that deviseth wicked imaginations, feet that be swift in running to mischief,
19 A false witness that speaketh lies, and he that soweth discord among brethren.

The question facing the Church is not whether previous generations should be respected or whether younger generations should preserve everything they inherited. The more important question concerns the faithfulness with which each generation handles the responsibility entrusted to them. Those who came before us had a responsibility to teach and demonstrate righteousness, and those who follow have a responsibility to examine what they received and continue building upon a sound biblical foundation. Neither responsibility can be fulfilled through unquestioning loyalty or unnecessary rejection.

A generation honors its spiritual inheritance not by preserving every practice without examination, but by remaining faithful to the Word of God that should have guided those practices from the beginning.
