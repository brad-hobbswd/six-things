The Seven Things

Chapter 6: When the Church Sows Discord

The Destruction of Unity Through Division, Manipulation, and Conflict

Unity is an important part of the Christian faith because the Church is called to represent a body of believers joined together through their relationship with Jesus Christ. Although members may differ in age, background, experience, personality, and understanding, their differences should not become an excuse for hostility or division. Scripture does not suggest that every believer must agree on every matter, but it does establish a responsibility to pursue peace, demonstrate humility, and preserve relationships through truth and love. When individuals deliberately cultivate conflict among others, they work against the very fellowship the Church is called to demonstrate.

Proverbs 6:19 identifies the person who sows discord among brethren as the seventh thing that the Lord hates. The choice of imagery is significant because sowing involves deliberately placing something into the ground with the expectation that growth will follow. In the same manner, discord can begin through words, attitudes, and actions that introduce suspicion and hostility into relationships. The consequences may not become immediately visible, but when division is repeatedly encouraged, the resulting damage can spread throughout an entire congregation.

Discord is not simply the presence of disagreement. Healthy congregations will experience differences of opinion, difficult conversations, and circumstances requiring correction. Such experiences do not automatically represent a failure of Christian unity. The concern arises when individuals deliberately encourage hostility, manipulate relationships, or attempt to create opposing sides within a community. Rather than seeking understanding or resolution, their conduct contributes to an environment where personal loyalty becomes more important than truth and relationships become governed by suspicion.

The apostle Paul addressed the seriousness of division in 1 Corinthians 1:10–13 when he appealed to believers to be united in mind and judgment rather than divided into competing groups. The Corinthian congregation had developed factions associated with different individuals, creating an environment where personal allegiance threatened the unity of the body. Paul’s response redirected their attention toward Jesus Christ, reminding them that the Church does not belong to individual personalities or human leaders. Its foundation and identity must remain centered upon Christ.

1 Corinthians 1:10–13
10 Now I beseech you, brethren, by the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, that ye all speak the same thing, and that there be no divisions among you; but that ye be perfectly joined together in the same mind and in the same judgment.
11 For it hath been declared unto me of you, my brethren, by them which are of the house of Chloe, that there are contentions among you.
12 Now this I say, that every one of you saith, I am of Paul; and I of Apollos; and I of Cephas; and I of Christ.
13 Is Christ divided? was Paul crucified for you? or were ye baptized in the name of Paul?

This principle is especially important within congregations where leadership personalities and longstanding relationships carry considerable influence. A church should never become an environment where access, acceptance, or belonging depends upon choosing sides in personal disagreements. Members should be able to participate in worship and fellowship without becoming entangled in conflicts that have little to do with the purpose of the congregation. Leadership must also recognize that unresolved disagreements can become more damaging when personal influence is used to strengthen divisions rather than encourage responsible communication.

Ephesians 4:1–6 provides further instruction concerning the preservation of unity. Paul encourages believers to walk with humility, gentleness, patience, and love, making every effort to maintain the unity of the Spirit through the bond of peace. These qualities are not presented as optional characteristics for particularly agreeable circumstances. They are part of the conduct expected from those who understand the significance of their shared faith. Unity requires more than attending the same services or identifying with the same denomination; it requires a willingness to treat one another according to the principles of Christ.

Ephesians 4:1–6
1 I therefore, the prisoner of the Lord, beseech you that ye walk worthy of the vocation wherewith ye are called,
2 With all lowliness and meekness, with longsuffering, forbearing one another in love;
3 Endeavouring to keep the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace.
4 There is one body, and one Spirit, even as ye are called in one hope of your calling;
5 One Lord, one faith, one baptism,
6 One God and Father of all, who is above all, and through all, and in you all.

However, preserving unity must never become an excuse for ignoring injustice, concealing misconduct, or discouraging legitimate accountability. There are circumstances in which difficult conversations are necessary, and there are situations in which leaders must address wrongdoing even when doing so creates discomfort. Biblical peace is not established through silence or the protection of appearances. Genuine peace requires truth, responsible action, and a commitment to righteousness. A person who raises a legitimate concern is not automatically creating division, just as someone who demands agreement is not automatically protecting unity.

Romans 12:18 provides an important balance by instructing believers, as much as possible and as far as circumstances depend upon them, to live peaceably with all people. The wording recognizes that peace requires personal responsibility while acknowledging that relationships involve the decisions and conduct of more than one individual. Believers can pursue reconciliation, communicate honestly, and demonstrate patience, but they cannot independently control the choices of everyone involved. The responsibility is to remain faithful to godly conduct regardless of how others respond.

Romans 12:18
18 If it be possible, as much as lieth in you, live peaceably with all men.

The responsibility to preserve unity also rests heavily upon those entrusted with leadership. Pastors, elders, deacons, and other influential members must recognize the effect their words and decisions can have upon the congregation. Their responsibilities should include encouraging honest communication, addressing conflict fairly, and refusing to use personal influence to create divisions. Leadership becomes a source of stability when decisions are guided by righteousness rather than personal alliances or the desire to maintain control.

The warning in Proverbs 6:19 is therefore particularly relevant to the Church because division can undermine the relationships that make meaningful fellowship possible. A congregation may continue holding services, organizing ministries, and maintaining its public identity while struggling with unresolved hostility among its members. The presence of religious activity alone does not establish the presence of genuine unity. The character of a congregation must also be considered through the way its members communicate, resolve disagreements, and respond when relationships become difficult.

The call to unity is ultimately a call to reflect the character of Christ in our relationships. Believers are not required to abandon conviction, overlook wrongdoing, or pretend that every disagreement has been resolved. They are required to pursue truth without malice, correction without humiliation, and peace without compromising righteousness. Such a commitment requires maturity from every generation and responsibility from everyone who participates in the life of a congregation.

A church cannot faithfully pursue the unity of Christ while deliberately cultivating the division that His Word condemns.
