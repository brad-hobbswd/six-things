The Seven Things

Chapter One: The Seven Things God Hates

Understanding the Weight of Solomon’s Words

Proverbs 6:16–19 presents one of the most direct examinations of human character found in the wisdom literature of Scripture. Solomon identifies seven behaviors that the Lord hates, describing conduct that contradicts His righteousness and damages the lives of those affected by such actions. The passage deserves careful attention because its message reaches beyond outward behavior and addresses the attitudes, intentions, and decisions that shape a person’s character.

Solomon begins, “These six things doth the LORD hate: Yea, seven are an abomination unto him.” This construction reflects a Hebrew literary pattern known as numerical parallelism, in which a number is introduced and then increased by one to emphasize the list that follows. Similar expressions appear elsewhere in Proverbs, particularly in chapters 30:15–16 and 30:18–19. Rather than limiting God’s displeasure to seven specific offenses, the expression draws attention to the seriousness of the behaviors being identified.

The word translated as abomination comes from the Hebrew term to’evah, which describes something detestable or deeply offensive. Solomon’s choice of language establishes that these behaviors are matters of serious moral concern. They are not simply differences in personality, disagreements between individuals, or occasional lapses in judgment. They represent conduct that stands against the character God desires His people to demonstrate.

The order of the passage is also meaningful. Solomon begins with what can be observed in a person’s appearance, moves through speech and physical actions, addresses the intentions of the heart, and concludes with conduct that damages relationships within a community. The progression provides a broad examination of human behavior, revealing that righteousness must influence both the inner life and outward conduct.

A Proud Look

The first thing Solomon identifies is a proud look, an expression associated with haughty eyes and an attitude of superiority. Pride changes how individuals perceive themselves and those around them, often creating a sense of importance that leaves little room for humility or correction. A proud person may consider their experience, knowledge, reputation, or position sufficient evidence that their judgment should remain unquestioned.

Within the biblical understanding of righteousness, humility is not weakness or a lack of confidence. Rather, humility recognizes that every person remains accountable to God and has room for growth. Proverbs 16:18 warns that pride goes before destruction and a haughty spirit before a fall. Solomon’s concern reaches beyond an outward expression of arrogance and addresses the inward disposition that elevates personal importance above godly instruction.

A Lying Tongue

The second thing is a lying tongue. Truthfulness is essential to relationships because trust depends upon the reliability of what people communicate. A lying tongue deliberately misrepresents reality, whether through fabricated statements, intentional omissions, or words designed to deceive.

Scripture repeatedly emphasizes the responsibility to speak truthfully. Ephesians 4:25 instructs believers to put away lying and speak truth with one another. This instruction establishes honesty as an essential characteristic of Christian conduct rather than an optional quality reserved for convenient circumstances. A person’s words should reflect integrity, particularly when those words concern another human being.

Hands That Shed Innocent Blood

The third thing addresses the shedding of innocent blood, identifying the deliberate destruction of human life as a grave offense against God’s righteousness. Human life carries significance because humanity was created in the image of God, as established in Genesis 1:26–27. The protection of innocent life is therefore a matter of divine concern rather than merely a social expectation.

Throughout Scripture, God condemns violence and injustice against those who are innocent or vulnerable. Proverbs 24:11–12 further emphasizes the responsibility to intervene when people are being led toward destruction. Solomon’s language reminds readers that righteousness includes recognizing the value of human life and accepting responsibility for protecting those who cannot adequately protect themselves.

A Heart That Deviseth Wicked Imaginations

The fourth thing moves beyond visible behavior and addresses the intentions formed within the heart. The Hebrew expression describes a person who deliberately plans wrongdoing, revealing that the concern is not limited to actions already committed. The thoughts, motives, and intentions that guide a person’s decisions matter to God.

In biblical wisdom literature, the heart represents the center of a person’s inner life, including reasoning, desires, intentions, and moral decisions. Proverbs 4:23 instructs readers to guard their hearts because the issues of life proceed from them. The principle is especially important when considering the difference between an unwanted temptation and a deliberate decision to cultivate harmful intentions. Solomon addresses the latter, identifying the calculated planning of wickedness as conduct that God finds detestable.

Feet That Be Swift in Running to Mischief

The fifth thing describes an eagerness to pursue wrongdoing. The imagery of swift feet suggests readiness and deliberate movement toward conduct that should instead be resisted. Solomon identifies a disposition in which wrongdoing is not merely tolerated but actively pursued.

This expression adds an important dimension to the preceding discussion. A person’s intentions eventually influence their decisions, and those decisions reveal the direction of their character. The individual described here demonstrates a willingness to participate in wrongdoing rather than exercise restraint or choose righteousness. Proverbs 1:15–16 similarly warns against following the paths of those whose feet run toward evil and hasten to shed blood.

A False Witness That Speaketh Lies

The sixth thing addresses false testimony, particularly statements presented as evidence against another person when those statements are untrue. Although closely related to the lying tongue, this expression carries a more specific concern because false testimony can influence judgments, establish wrongful conclusions, and expose innocent people to serious consequences.

The importance of truthful testimony is established throughout the Old Testament. The ninth commandment in Exodus 20:16 expressly prohibits bearing false witness against one’s neighbor. Deuteronomy 19:15–19 further addresses the examination of testimony and the consequences of deliberate false accusation. These instructions demonstrate that truthfulness is inseparable from justice and that accusations must be treated with appropriate seriousness.

A false witness does more than communicate dishonesty; such a person uses testimony to distort the truth concerning another individual. Solomon identifies this behavior as especially offensive because of the injustice that follows when falsehood is presented as truth.

He That Soweth Discord Among Brethren

The seventh and final thing addresses the deliberate creation of division among people. Solomon uses the imagery of sowing to describe how discord can be introduced and cultivated within relationships. Just as planted seed produces growth, words and actions intended to create hostility can develop into lasting conflict.

The reference to brethren is particularly significant because the passage addresses relationships within a community. God’s expectations concerning His people extend beyond individual conduct to the way they live together. Unity requires truthfulness, humility, mutual consideration, and a willingness to pursue peace without abandoning righteousness.

Psalm 133:1 declares, “Behold, how good and how pleasant it is for brethren to dwell together in unity.” This principle establishes unity as something worthy of preservation. However, biblical unity does not require concealing wrongdoing or avoiding legitimate correction. Genuine unity must remain grounded in truth and a shared commitment to the principles of God.

The Weight of the Seven

Taken together, these seven behaviors reveal the breadth of God’s concern for human character. Solomon addresses pride, dishonesty, violence, malicious intentions, eagerness toward wrongdoing, false testimony, and the deliberate destruction of unity. Each expression identifies conduct that contradicts the righteousness God expects from those who claim to walk according to His instruction.

The significance of this passage becomes especially apparent when we consider that none of these behaviors is limited to a particular generation, religious position, denomination, or social environment. They are human behaviors that can appear wherever individuals allow personal desires, selfish ambitions, and disregard for truth to influence their decisions. Consequently, no person should approach this passage as though its message belongs exclusively to someone else’s examination.

Religious knowledge does not automatically produce righteous character, and years of service do not eliminate the responsibility to live according to the principles being taught. The authority of Scripture remains the same regardless of who is reading, teaching, or applying its message. Every believer, regardless of position or experience, remains responsible for examining personal conduct against the Word of God.

The seven things God hates are not seven accusations to direct toward others, but seven serious warnings that every believer must be willing to examine within their own life.
