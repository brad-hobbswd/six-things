Chapter 5: The Tongue That Destroys a Reputation

The Consequences of Dishonesty, False Accusations, and Words Without Truth

Words carry a responsibility that should never be taken lightly. They can communicate truth, establish understanding, encourage those experiencing difficulty, and provide testimony concerning matters that require attention. However, words can also distort circumstances, damage reputations, and influence how others perceive someone whose side of a situation has never been properly heard. Scripture repeatedly addresses the power of speech because the tongue reveals something about the condition of the heart. Proverbs 6:17 identifies a lying tongue as something the Lord hates, while verse 19 identifies a false witness that speaks lies. Although these descriptions are closely connected, they address distinct expressions of dishonesty that deserve individual consideration.

A lying tongue represents the broader practice of deliberately communicating what is untrue. Dishonesty can occur in ordinary conversations, personal relationships, professional settings, and religious communities. Some lies are told to avoid responsibility, others are used to protect personal interests, and some are intended to influence how another person is viewed. Regardless of the motivation, deliberate deception damages trust because those receiving the information are being asked to accept a version of reality that the speaker knows is inaccurate. Proverbs 12:22 declares that lying lips are an abomination to the Lord, while those who deal truly are His delight. This contrast establishes that honesty is not merely a desirable social quality but a matter of spiritual integrity.

Proverbs 12:22 · KJV
22 Lying lips are abomination to the LORD: but they that deal truly are his delight.

False testimony carries an additional responsibility because the dishonesty is directed toward establishing a claim about another person. In biblical society, testimony could influence judgments and determine whether someone was held responsible for an offense. The ninth commandment, recorded in Exodus 20:16, specifically prohibits bearing false witness against one’s neighbor. This command protects more than the accuracy of conversation; it establishes a moral responsibility to preserve truth when another person’s character, conduct, or circumstances are being evaluated. A false witness can turn personal hostility into an apparent matter of evidence, placing another individual in a position where their reputation and relationships may be affected by statements that do not reflect the truth.

Exodus 20:16 · KJV
16 Thou shalt not bear false witness against thy neighbour.

The distinction becomes especially important when accusations arise within religious communities. Churches are places where trust, personal relationships, and spiritual leadership frequently intersect, creating circumstances in which information can carry considerable influence. A statement made by a respected member may receive immediate acceptance because of the person’s reputation or position, even when the circumstances have not been adequately examined. However, familiarity with someone does not establish the accuracy of their testimony, and a respected title does not replace the responsibility to establish facts. Truth must be examined consistently, regardless of the identity of the person making a statement.

The Old Testament provides a clear example of the seriousness of false testimony through the account of Naboth in 1 Kings 21. King Ahab desired Naboth’s vineyard, but Naboth refused to surrender his ancestral inheritance. Jezebel subsequently arranged for false witnesses to accuse Naboth of cursing God and the king, resulting in his wrongful execution. The account demonstrates how deliberate dishonesty, when supported by influence and authority, can produce devastating consequences for an innocent person. The narrative is not simply about a dishonest statement; it reveals the injustice that can occur when testimony is manipulated to accomplish an outcome that those responsible already desire.

The seriousness of this principle remains relevant because an accusation should never be treated as an established fact merely because someone has spoken with confidence. Responsible examination requires attention to evidence, context, consistency, and the opportunity for those involved to be heard. Deuteronomy 19:15 establishes the importance of multiple witnesses in determining a matter, while Proverbs 18:17 reminds readers that the first person presenting a case may appear convincing until another comes forward and examines the matter. Together, these passages demonstrate the importance of discernment rather than immediate acceptance of the first account received.

Proverbs 18:17 · KJV
17 He that is first in his own cause seemeth just; but his neighbour cometh and searcheth him.
Deuteronomy 19:15 · KJV
15 One witness shall not rise up against a man for any iniquity, or for any sin, in any sin that he sinneth: at the mouth of two witnesses, or at the mouth of three witnesses, shall the matter be established.

Within church culture, the consequences of dishonesty can extend far beyond the original conversation. An inaccurate statement may influence friendships, ministry relationships, leadership decisions, and the willingness of others to associate with someone. When information is repeatedly communicated without verification, the distinction between what happened, what someone believes happened, and what has merely been repeated can become increasingly difficult to recognize. The responsibility to speak truth therefore includes the responsibility to exercise restraint when the facts remain uncertain.

There is also a difference between reporting genuine misconduct and participating in false accusation. People must remain free to raise legitimate concerns, report wrongdoing, and seek protection when circumstances require intervention. A demand for silence should never be used to shield misconduct or preserve the reputation of an institution. At the same time, allegations must be communicated honestly and handled through responsible procedures that protect everyone involved. Accountability loses its integrity when truth is sacrificed, regardless of which person or organization benefits from the distortion.

James 3:5–10 provides a sobering reminder that the tongue, although small, can influence matters of considerable consequence. James compares the tongue to a small fire capable of setting a great forest ablaze, illustrating how uncontrolled speech can produce damage far beyond the original words. His teaching calls believers to recognize the responsibility attached to communication and to understand that worship and destructive speech cannot comfortably coexist. The words we speak should reflect the character of the God we claim to serve.

James 3:5–10 · KJV
5 Even so the tongue is a little member, and boasteth great things. Behold, how great a matter a little fire kindleth!
6 And the tongue is a fire, a world of iniquity: so is the tongue among our members, that it defileth the whole body, and setteth on fire the course of nature; and it is set on fire of hell.
7 For every kind of beasts, and of birds, and of serpents, and of things in the sea, is tamed, and hath been tamed of mankind:
8 But the tongue can no man tame; it is an unruly evil, full of deadly poison.
9 Therewith bless we God, even the Father; and therewith curse we men, which are made after the similitude of God.
10 Out of the same mouth proceedeth blessing and cursing. My brethren, these things ought not so to be.

The Church must therefore maintain a commitment to truth that extends beyond public preaching and religious instruction. Such a commitment requires honesty in private conversations, fairness when evaluating accusations, and restraint when information has not been established. It also requires the courage to correct false statements when they have been discovered and the willingness to acknowledge personal responsibility when our own words have caused harm.

A person’s reputation should never become an object of entertainment, personal retaliation, or careless conversation. Truthful accountability may require difficult discussions, but those discussions must remain grounded in facts and conducted with integrity. Proverbs 6 makes clear that both the lying tongue and the false witness are offensive to God, reminding us that the responsibility to speak truthfully remains essential wherever human relationships are involved.

Before speaking about another person’s character, we must be willing to answer for the truthfulness of our own words.
