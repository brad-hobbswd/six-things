The Seven Things

Chapter 8: The Generational Wounds We Inherited

When Harmful Practices Become Part of a Spiritual Legacy

Spiritual and emotional wounds can remain present long after the original experience has ended. A person may leave a congregation, move into a different community, or establish a new family while still carrying expectations formed through earlier experiences. Some inherited lessons provide stability and belonging; others shape fear, silence, distrust, or difficulty receiving correction. This chapter considers those lasting effects and the choices that can interrupt their continuation.

The effects of painful religious experiences are not always immediately recognizable. A person may interpret ordinary disagreement as rejection, struggle to trust spiritual leadership, or feel responsible for keeping peace at the expense of honest communication. These responses deserve patience and careful understanding rather than quick judgments about faith or character.

Scripture recognizes the influence of one generation upon another and repeatedly emphasizes the responsibility to teach future generations the ways of God. Psalm 78:4–7 describes the importance of declaring God’s works to children so that succeeding generations may establish their hope in Him and remain committed to His commandments. This responsibility involves more than communicating religious information. Children and younger believers also observe the conduct of those teaching them, making personal example an important part of spiritual instruction. A message about humility carries greater meaning when demonstrated through humble conduct, just as instruction concerning honesty becomes more credible when accompanied by truthful behavior.

Psalm 78:4–7 We will not hide them from their children, shewing to the generation to come the praises of the LORD, and his strength, and his wonderful works that he hath done. For he established a testimony in Jacob, and appointed a law in Israel, which he commanded our fathers, that they should make them known to their children: That the generation to come might know them, even the children which should be born; who should arise and declare them to their children: That they might set their hope in God, and not forget the works of God, but keep his commandments:

The responsibility to teach the next generation is also described in Deuteronomy as a daily responsibility woven into ordinary life. The instruction was not limited to formal gatherings or religious ceremonies. God’s words were to be kept in the heart and taught diligently to children through the rhythms of home and community. The passage makes the conduct of those teaching inseparable from the faith being handed down. A spiritual inheritance is shaped not only by what a generation says, but by what children repeatedly see practiced.

Deuteronomy 6:6–7 And these words, which I command thee this day, shall be in thine heart: And thou shalt teach them diligently unto thy children, and shalt talk of them when thou sittest in thine house, and when thou walkest by the way, and when thou liest down, and when thou risest up.

The difficulty arises when harmful practices become so familiar that they are mistaken for necessary expressions of tradition. A congregation may continue certain expectations because previous leaders established them, even when those expectations no longer serve a sound biblical purpose. Some members may feel obligated to preserve practices simply because questioning them appears disrespectful to those who introduced them. However, the preservation of faith does not require the preservation of every human decision made throughout a church’s history. The responsibility of each generation is to remain faithful to God’s Word rather than automatically defend everything received from the past.

The seven things identified in Proverbs 6:16–19 provide a serious framework for considering the consequences of such inherited behavior. Pride can become established through an environment where influential individuals are rarely challenged, dishonesty can continue when uncomfortable truths are consistently concealed, and discord can become familiar when personal conflicts are repeatedly passed from one group of members to another. These behaviors do not become acceptable through repetition, nor does their familiarity diminish their seriousness before God. A practice that has continued for decades still deserves examination when its consequences contradict biblical righteousness.

There is also a responsibility to acknowledge the circumstances that shaped previous generations. Many traditional congregations developed within communities experiencing economic hardship, social exclusion, discrimination, and limited institutional support. The Church frequently served as a place of refuge, organization, education, mutual assistance, and spiritual encouragement. Those experiences influenced the expectations and practices of many congregations, sometimes creating strong boundaries around community identity and leadership. Understanding this history helps explain why certain traditions became important, while also reminding us that historical circumstances should inform our examination rather than excuse harmful conduct.

Healing generational wounds requires more than identifying what previous generations did incorrectly. It requires a willingness to understand how those behaviors affected people and why they continued. Some individuals may have repeated practices they inherited without recognizing their consequences, while others may have understood the harm but lacked the courage or opportunity to challenge established expectations. Recognizing these differences allows the conversation to remain fair and thoughtful rather than assigning identical motives to everyone involved.

The responsibility for change belongs to the generation that recognizes the need for it. Parents, pastors, elders, and longtime members must consider what their words and actions communicate to those who follow them. Younger believers must also recognize that they have a responsibility to establish healthier patterns rather than simply reacting against everything associated with previous generations. Neither age nor experience guarantees spiritual maturity. Meaningful progress requires every generation to remain teachable and willing to correct practices that contradict the teachings of Christ.

Second Timothy 2:2 provides a valuable principle concerning the transmission of faith. Paul instructs Timothy to entrust what he has received to faithful people who will also be capable of teaching others. The passage emphasizes the importance of passing along sound instruction through individuals whose lives and character can support the responsibility they have received. A healthy spiritual legacy therefore depends upon more than preserving an institution or maintaining familiar customs. It requires the continued transmission of truth through people committed to living according to that truth.

2 Timothy 2:2 And the things that thou hast heard of me among many witnesses, the same commit thou to faithful men, who shall be able to teach others also.

The Church must also recognize that forgiveness and reconciliation do not require pretending that harmful experiences never occurred. People may need opportunities to speak honestly about what they experienced, and congregations may need to acknowledge decisions or practices that caused unnecessary harm. Such conversations should be handled with fairness, discretion, and a genuine commitment to restoration. Acknowledgment creates an opportunity for understanding, while accountability provides a foundation for meaningful change.

The inheritance of faith should be measured by the truth we preserve, the character we demonstrate, and the responsibility we accept toward those who follow us. Previous generations deserve recognition for the good they contributed, but their contributions should not prevent necessary correction. Younger generations deserve opportunities to grow without being required to repeat harmful patterns simply to prove their respect for tradition.

A spiritual legacy is worth preserving when the truth of God’s Word remains stronger than the traditions, attitudes, and behaviors that generations have carried forward.
