The message behind the pages

About the Book

When Scripture Becomes a Weapon and Religion Forgets Its Own Reflection

What happens when the very Scripture meant to transform our hearts becomes a weapon used to judge someone else's?

In Proverbs 6:16–19, Solomon identifies seven things that the Lord hates. These verses confront pride, dishonesty, injustice, wicked intentions, eagerness to pursue wrongdoing, false testimony, and the deliberate sowing of discord. Their message reaches far beyond outward appearances or familiar religious expectations. They expose the condition of the human heart.

In The Seven Things, Bradley Hobbs invites readers to examine how these behaviors can become embedded in religious culture, inherited traditions, spiritual leadership, and relationships within the Church. The book explores what happens when respected positions go unquestioned, traditions take precedence over biblical truth, and Scripture becomes a means of condemnation rather than a call to repentance.

Through biblical examination, historical reflection, and challenging questions of spiritual responsibility, Hobbs addresses the tension between religious appearance and genuine Christian character. He recognizes the faithful contributions of earlier generations while challenging every generation to examine the practices and attitudes passed forward.

This book is not an indictment of the Church, an attack on older generations, or a rejection of biblical holiness. Rather, the message calls believers of every age and position to embrace the accountability demanded by the very Word they profess.

The seven things should never become a convenient list of accusations against others. They should first become a mirror through which we examine ourselves.

Before pointing your finger, allow the Word to examine your heart.
