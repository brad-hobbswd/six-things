The Seven Things

Closing Thoughts

The Word Was Never the Problem

The seven things described in Proverbs 6:16–19 leave us with a lasting reminder that God is concerned with the character of those who claim to represent Him. Pride, dishonesty, malicious intentions, injustice, false testimony, and division have no rightful place in a life devoted to righteousness. Their presence deserves attention wherever they appear, including within the institutions and communities established in the name of Jesus Christ.

Proverbs 6:16–19 These six things doth the LORD hate: yea, seven are an abomination unto him: A proud look, a lying tongue, and hands that shed innocent blood, An heart that deviseth wicked imaginations, feet that be swift in running to mischief, A false witness that speaketh lies, and he that soweth discord among brethren.

The history of the Church contains examples of extraordinary faithfulness alongside circumstances that deserve honest examination. We should preserve the testimonies of those who prayed, sacrificed, served, and carried the faith through difficult seasons. We should also acknowledge the experiences of those who encountered harmful practices and recognize the responsibility to establish healthier patterns for those who follow. Honoring our spiritual inheritance requires both gratitude and discernment.

Jesus connected freedom with continuing in His word. In John 8:31–32, He spoke to those who believed on Him and described discipleship as continuing in His teaching, with the result that they would know the truth. The connection is essential: truth is not a slogan to be claimed while conduct remains unchanged. The Word is to be received, continued in, and allowed to shape the lives of those who follow Christ. This is how the message remains living among the living, in spirit and in truth.

John 8:31–32 Then said Jesus to those Jews which believed on him, If ye continue in my word, then are ye my disciples indeed; And ye shall know the truth, and the truth shall make you free.

Perhaps the greatest lesson of this examination is that Scripture must never become a substitute for personal transformation. Knowing the passages, remembering the commandments, and teaching others about righteousness carry little meaning when our own conduct contradicts the principles we proclaim. The Word of God was never intended to become a collection of weapons for personal disputes. Its authority calls us toward humility, truth, justice, and a life that reflects the character of Christ.

Every generation has an opportunity to make a meaningful contribution to the spiritual future of the Church. We can preserve what is good, correct what has become harmful, and establish a legacy grounded in biblical integrity. Such responsibility belongs to longtime believers and new converts, pastors and congregations, parents and children alike.

The seven things should remain a mirror before they ever become an accusation.

Let the Word examine you first.
