Historical Sources and References

The historical discussion in Chapter Nine draws attention to the complicated relationship between Christian institutions, religious authority, racial discrimination, slavery, and the development of independent Black churches. The sources below provide historical context and scholarly documentation for those discussions.

The purpose of including these references is to distinguish documented history from personal interpretation. The Church’s historical record includes both examples of institutional injustice and examples of courageous believers who challenged injustice through their faith.

Books and Scholarly Publications

  • Bonar, Chance E. “Early Christianity and Slavery.” Oxford Bibliographies in Biblical Studies, 2024. Oxford University Press. Oxford Academic. This scholarly overview examines slavery in the Roman world and its relationship to early Christian writings and communities.
  • Glancy, Jennifer A. Slavery in Early Christianity. Oxford University Press, 2002. Oxford Academic. A scholarly examination of slavery in the social and religious world of early Christianity.
  • Gerbner, Katharine. “Church and Slavery.” Oxford Bibliographies in Atlantic History, 2022. Oxford University Press. Oxford Academic. This reference examines Christian participation in slavery, competing religious arguments, and the role of enslaved and free Black Christians in antislavery movements.
  • Gutacker, Paul J. “Seventeen Centuries of Sin: The Christian Past in Antebellum Slavery Debates.” Church History, vol. 89, no. 2, 2020, pp. 307–332. Cambridge University Press. An examination of how opposing sides in American slavery debates used Christian history and religious tradition to support their arguments.
  • Sommar, Mary E. The Slaves of the Churches: A History. Oxford University Press, 2020. Oxford Academic. A historical study of slavery involving Christian institutions, church authorities, and ecclesiastical law.

Historical Archives and Institutional Records

  • The Archives of the Episcopal Church. “Richard Allen, 1760–1831.” The Church Awakens: African Americans and the Struggle for Justice. Episcopal Archives. Historical documentation of Richard Allen’s life, his response to racial discrimination in Methodist worship, and the development of the African Methodist Episcopal Church.
  • General Commission on Archives and History. “Richard Allen, 1760–1831.” United Methodist Church. United Methodist Church. A biographical historical account of Allen’s life, ministry, leadership, and role in the founding of the African Methodist Episcopal Church.
  • African Methodist Episcopal Church, Tenth Episcopal District. “Our History.” AMEC 10th Episcopal District. An institutional history documenting the formation of the African Methodist Episcopal Church in 1816 and the events leading to its organization.
  • Blumhofer, Edith. “Azusa Street Revival: Historiography of Pentecostalism.” The Christian Century, March 7, 2006. The Christian Century. An examination of the Azusa Street Revival, William J. Seymour’s leadership, interracial participation, and the revival’s influence on the development of global Pentecostalism.

Editorial and Research Note

This bibliography establishes a starting foundation for the historical claims in Chapter Nine. Before publication, the chapter should receive a final source audit so every specific historical claim is matched to the appropriate source, page number, archival record, or primary document. Additional primary sources should be included wherever the manuscript discusses particular denominational decisions, historical figures, or women’s leadership in Pentecostal movements.

The historical record deserves careful treatment. The goal is neither to erase the faithful contributions of earlier generations nor to excuse the harm committed under religious authority. Both must be examined honestly.
